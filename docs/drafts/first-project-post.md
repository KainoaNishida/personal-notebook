I have a handful of things I want to make time for: going to the gym, reading research papers, studying topics like system design, drawing portraits, and reading. Some days I'll do several of them. Other days I'll spend most of my time on one.

The habit I want to keep every day is journaling. I want a place to record what I did, reflect on what I learned, and see which interests I've spent time on lately.

That's the purpose of this project: a personal note-taking app built around my own workflow.

## A notebook for each kind of work

The plan is to keep lasting notebooks for different subjects, with dated entries and a Today page that brings the day together. I like the Markdown feel of Obsidian, and I want writing to stay straightforward.

Each notebook should also fit its subject. An art journal should let me put my drawings and reference images alongside prose, with text wrapping around them. A science notebook needs room for the paper itself, equations, and notes that stay connected to their sources.

## Reading papers with the source in view

The science workspace is the first specialized workflow I want to build. The PDF will sit on the left and my notebook on the right. Highlighting a passage should let me create a link in my notes that brings me back to that exact part of the paper.

I also want help with the moments where an equation or analytical argument slows me down. I'll select the material and explicitly ask for an explanation. The explanation should unpack the notation, walk through the steps, and build intuition, with the option to insert it into my notes and edit it.

That help should also be visual when useful: an equation broken into labeled parts, a diagram of how information flows, or another representation that makes an idea easier to understand. I want to embed my own photos and diagrams too. AI-created explanations should stay connected to the source material, and they should only run when I ask.

I'm studying computer science and mathematics, so calculus, linear algebra, and algorithms are a starting point. But knowing those fundamentals doesn't mean every unfamiliar derivation is obvious. The tool should help fill those gaps when I ask.

## Turning reflection into something shareable

After the initial journaling and science workflow, the next feature will connect this app to my portfolio. I want to select entries from a day, turn them into an editable post, and highlight the takeaways worth sharing. The app will submit a GitHub pull request so I can review it before it goes on the site.

This post is the starting point: why I'm building the project and what I hope it will help me do. At this stage, I've defined the requirements and priorities; the application itself is still to be built.

## What matters most

- Make the journal pleasant enough that I want to return to it every day.
- Let goals stay flexible while keeping a record of what I actually did.
- Keep scientific explanations connected to the source and under my control.
- Make sharing selected learning easy while keeping the rest of the journal private.

There are ideas for later, including assistance with gym research and exploring how WHOOP data relates to my routines. For now, the focus is a useful daily journal, a better paper-reading workflow, and a way to share what I'm learning.
